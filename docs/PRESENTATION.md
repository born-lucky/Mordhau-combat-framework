# Framework presentation

The separate framework application enables authored training visuals by default.
Steel, ivory, and brass capsules, spheres, and simple blade/guard/grip primitives
are generated by our code. No character or weapon artwork is embedded in them.

Characters follow the existing BodyJoints pose, including physical death output.
Weapons use current normal/alternate trace sockets through the drawn held-component
transform. They are generic training silhouettes, not original weapon models or
new weapon collision shapes. Missing poses/sockets hide the visual instead of
inventing an origin. The original asset readers remain available locally.

Our first-person figure shows arms and hands. Native locally imported shadow
proxies retain their existing first-person behavior; visible authored arms do not
cast a second floating-arm shadow. Third-person figures cast their authored
silhouette; first-person deaths retain the native proxies. This is a presentation choice, not a claim of matching
all original first-person body visibility or artwork.

The health and stamina bars read the controlled fighter's live stat values and
per-fighter minimum/maximum bounds. Regeneration, costs, damage, timings, poses,
and collision remain simulation responsibilities. The help legend uses saved input
bindings. Original match draw roots are hidden while our HUD is displayed; original
menu logic remains available. Full menu artwork replacement is future work.

The corrected parry-effect source uses the defender's current weapon trace and raw
camera fields before cosmetic offsets, including the native first-person flag.
Attached emitters follow their weapon while previously born world-space particles
stay fixed. Particle bounds are refreshed after movement. Native presentation math
is available to hosts through mh-framework::presentation.

Remaining effect gaps include shields/left-hand attachment, motion OverrideTrace,
native effect budgets, local-space attached emitters, blood collision receivers,
and dismemberment/gore. Particle shaders translated from original data must remain
in the user's local cache; they are not bundled in this repository. The resolver
uses that configured cache rather than a developer build path.

Source compilation and pure presentation-math tests have passed during staging.
Rendered candidate validation and clean consumer setup are separate acceptance
checks. A developer-input capture, if made, must not be described as a clean-install
or redistributable-package test.
